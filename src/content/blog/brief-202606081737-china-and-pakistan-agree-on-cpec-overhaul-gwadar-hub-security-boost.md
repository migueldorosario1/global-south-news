<p>BEIJING — China and Pakistan unveiled a “new broad consensus” on Tuesday to overhaul the strategic China-Pakistan Economic Corridor, position the Arabian Sea port of Gwadar as a regional trade hub, and invite third-party participation in the multibillion-dollar infrastructure network. The agreement was sealed as Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif wrapped up talks with President Xi Jinping and Premier Li Qiang, amid Islamabad’s push for foreign investment while it grapples with tensions along its Afghan border and mediates in the Iran conflict.</p>

<p>A joint statement published after the meetings outlined plans to upgrade the Khunjerab Pass and the Karakoram Highway — the lifeline linking western China to the Arabian Sea — and to accelerate “high-quality” development of CPEC, a flagship of Beijing’s Belt and Road Initiative. Crucially, Islamabad offered targeted steps to bolster security for Chinese nationals and projects on Pakistani soil, addressing a persistent irritant that has seen fatal militant attacks on Chinese workers in recent years.</p>

<p>The communique said both sides “welcomed third parties to participate in the development of the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor under the model agreed,” a move that could draw Gulf or other international capital into the project. The transformation of Gwadar into a connectivity hub is expected to spur regional trade and deepen economic integration across South and Central Asia.</p>

<p>On regional diplomacy, Beijing praised Pakistan’s role in securing the temporary U.S.-Iran ceasefire and holding talks in Islamabad, and the two countries reiterated support for a five-point initiative to restore peace in West Asia. They further opposed the use of territory by militant groups — including the Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan and the Eastern Turkistan Islamic Movement — to imperil regional security or launch attacks, and Pakistan welcomed China’s efforts to promote dialogue with Kabul.</p>

<p>Pakistan also reaffirmed its commitment to the one-China principle, calling Taiwan an “inalienable” part of China and opposing any form of independence.</p>
