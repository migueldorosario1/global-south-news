<p>Faced with the threat of default and weighed down by a massive debt burden, a new government in Senegal has made reopening negotiations with the International Monetary Fund (IMF) its priority, with the aim of reaching an agreement before June 30. Leaders Bassirou Diomaye Faye and Ousmane Sonko, who rose to power in 2024 on a platform of economic sovereignty, institutional reform, and transparent governance, are now confronting the harsh realities of economic restructuring, according to the news programme Business Africa.</p><p>Economist Abdoulaye Ndiaye, recently named Best Young African Economist at the Africa CEO Forum in Kigali, outlined three key reforms that he said could help pull Senegal out of the crisis and prevent it from escalating into something far more serious.</p>
