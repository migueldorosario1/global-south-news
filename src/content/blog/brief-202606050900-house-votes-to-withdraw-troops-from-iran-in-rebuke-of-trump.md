<p>WASHINGTON, June 4 – The U.S. House of Representatives voted 215-208 on Wednesday to order the withdrawal of American troops from the Iran war, with four Republicans joining Democrats in a rare bipartisan rebuke of President Donald Trump’s military campaign.</p>
<p>Trump denounced the vote on Thursday as “unpatriotic,” claiming it disrupted his final negotiations with Tehran to end the three-month-old conflict. “Who would do such an unpatriotic thing. They know where the negotiations stand,” he wrote on his Truth Social platform.</p>
<p>The measure, which now heads to the Senate, marks the first time the Republican-controlled House has approved legislation seeking to force Trump to wind down operations against Iran since the war began. The bill is largely symbolic, as Trump is certain to veto it, and backers lack the two-thirds majority to override.</p>
<p>Democrats accuse Trump of violating the Constitution by ordering strikes on Iran alongside Israel in late February without congressional authorisation. Under the War Powers Act, presidents must obtain legislative approval within 60 days of introducing forces into hostilities—a deadline that passed weeks ago, leaving Trump technically in breach of the law, they argue.</p>
<p>Trump lashed out at both parties. “(Democrats) would rather have our Country fail than give me another, of many, victories,” he said. Addressing the four Republican defectors, he added: “They’re GRANDSTANDERS! They should be ashamed of themselves.”</p>
<p>The vote underscores escalating congressional resistance to Trump’s West Asia military actions and sets up a potential constitutional showdown over war powers, even as the conflict’s outcome remains uncertain.</p>
