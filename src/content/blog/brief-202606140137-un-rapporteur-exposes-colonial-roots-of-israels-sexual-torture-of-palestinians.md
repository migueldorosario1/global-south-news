<p>Israel’s systematic sexual torture of Palestinian prisoners is a direct inheritance of British and French colonial counterinsurgency methods, according to Francesca Albanese, the UN Special Rapporteur on the occupied Palestinian territories. “Torture is not new to the Palestinians. Israel has practised torture against the Palestinians since the very beginning of its existence,” she said.</p>
<p>Albanese traced the genealogy in an Al Jazeera documentary, “Bodies of Evidence: Israel’s Darkest Weapon”, arguing that techniques documented in recent testimonies — including rape with objects, genital electrocution, forced nudity and threats against relatives — were first imported by former British Black and Tans deployed to Mandate Palestine in 1922. Up to 95 percent of the British Palestine Gendarmerie had served in Ireland’s war of independence, bringing with them methods of humiliation and bodily coercion honed against the Irish insurgency. The emergency regulations Britain imposed on Palestine were later “immediately received and ingrained in the Israeli system,” Albanese added.</p>
<p>Abdel Latif Ghaith, a former detainee and later director of the prisoner-rights group Addameer, recalled witnessing the 1969 interrogation of Rasmea Odeh, who was raped with a stick and subjected to electric shocks to her mouth and genitals. Odeh’s father was brought into the room to break her. Albanese stated that such torture has been practised since the founding of Israel. Kifaya Khraim, international advocacy coordinator at the Women’s Centre for Legal Aid and Counselling in Ramallah, said Israeli forces deliberately exploit the social stigma attached to sexual violence.</p>
<p>Cuno Tarfusser, a former judge at the International Criminal Court, told the documentary that sexual crimes “become a method to fight the war.” The documentary further notes that this colonial chain of sexual violence — built by Britain, refined by France in Algeria and systematised by apartheid South Africa — was inherited by Israel, with survivors and advocates maintaining that international legal accountability remains absent.</p>
