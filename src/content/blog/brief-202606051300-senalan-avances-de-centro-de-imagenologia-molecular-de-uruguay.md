<p>Uruguay’s Center for Molecular Imaging (Cudim) is advancing a new radiopharmaceutical for the early diagnosis of Alzheimer’s disease, as the institution pushes ahead with 40 research projects aimed at detecting complex illnesses sooner, the Presidency reported.</p><p>According to the Presidency, the Alzheimer’s study has cleared the ethics committee and will begin human trials after a successful pre-clinical phase. Director General Alarico Rodríguez said the project brings together the Faculty of Medicine, the Hospital de Clínicas and the Academic Geriatrics Unit, linking the national imaging hub with core clinical and ageing expertise.</p><p>Cudim, founded in 2011, employs 350 professionals and has become a regional reference in oncology diagnostics, notably through its work on PET tracers for prostate cancer and other tumours. The Presidency detailed that 27 of the centre’s current investigations are fully underway, while the remaining 13 are under review by ethics committees and funding bodies at home and abroad.</p><p>The centre operates the only animal imaging equipment in Latin America with capabilities comparable to human-use technology, a strategic asset for translational research that accelerates the move from laboratory to patient. Officials also highlighted the strong presence of women scientists within the facility, which works closely with the University of the Republic and public health agencies.</p><p>The Presidency described the Alzheimer’s radiopharmaceutical as a milestone for the neurosciences portfolio, which harnesses the same infrastructure that has made Cudim a leading molecular imaging centre. The compound is designed to detect neurodegenerative disease at an earlier stage, potentially broadening access to diagnosis.</p>
