<p>China and Pakistan have agreed to revitalise the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor and transform the Gwadar port into a regional hub, as the two sides reached a “new broad consensus” to deepen strategic ties during Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif’s visit to Beijing.</p><p>In a joint statement released on Tuesday, after Sharif met President Xi Jinping and Premier Li Qiang, both countries committed to “high-quality” development of the flagship Belt and Road project. Plans include upgrading the Karakoram Highway, the main overland link between the two nations, and modernising the Khunjerab Pass crossing. Significantly, Beijing and Islamabad also explicitly welcomed the participation of third parties in CPEC under an agreed model, a move that could broaden the corridor’s investor base beyond Chinese state-backed entities.</p><p>Addressing one of China’s most urgent concerns, Pakistan pledged to take targeted steps to boost security for Chinese workers and investments, which have suffered repeated militant attacks. The commitment aims to reassure Islamabad’s chief economic partner as it seeks to attract greater investment.</p><p>The statement also highlighted Pakistan’s diplomatic role in mediating the temporary ceasefire between the US and Iran, with China commending those efforts. Pakistan reiterated its support for the one-China principle, describing Taiwan as an “inalienable” part of China and opposing any form of independence. Both sides opposed the use of territory by militant groups, including the Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan and Eastern Turkistan Islamic Movement.</p>
