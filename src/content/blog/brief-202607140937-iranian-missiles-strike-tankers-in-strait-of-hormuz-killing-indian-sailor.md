<p>One Indian crew member was killed and eight others were wounded when Iranian cruise missiles struck two Emirati oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, the United Arab Emirates Ministry of Defence said. The attack marks a sharp escalation in the strategic waterway, which has become a flashpoint since February.</p>

<p>The ministry said the tankers Mombasa and Al Bahiyah were targeted in the southern lane of the strait while navigating Omani territorial waters. The deceased was aboard the Mombasa; among the eight wounded, six were Indian nationals and two were Ukrainian. Four sustained serious injuries. Fires that broke out on both vessels were later brought under control.</p>

<p>The UAE condemned what it called a “blatant attack” and said it retained “its full right to respond to this escalation”. The ministry added that the country was taking all necessary measures to deal with any threats to its security and stability. Iran has not commented on the incident.</p>

<p>The strike comes amid weeks of heightened tension after the United States and Israel launched attacks on Iran on February 28. On Monday, President Donald Trump said the US was reinstating its blockade of Iranian shipping in the Gulf and would keep the strait open — for a fee — while warning of further strikes. Iran’s top joint military command responded that Washington had no role in determining the future of the waterway.</p>

<p>The conflict has thrown into doubt an interim US-Iranian agreement signed last month to reopen the strait and halt hostilities. Before the war, roughly a fifth of the world’s oil and gas traffic passed through Hormuz daily, delivering more than 15 million barrels of fuel to global markets.</p>

<p>Separately, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency reported that a tanker was hit by an unknown projectile 40 nautical miles northeast of Oman’s Qalhat, with the crew reported safe. It was not immediately clear whether the reports referred to the same incident.</p>
