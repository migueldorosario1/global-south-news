<p>An Al Jazeera investigation has traced the systematic sexual torture of Palestinian detainees by Israel to a direct colonial lineage inherited from British and French counterinsurgency methods. UN Special Rapporteur on the occupied Palestinian territories Francesca Albanese said the practices were “immediately received and ingrained in the Israeli system” from British Mandate emergency regulations, which themselves replicated tactics used against Irish insurgents.</p>
<p>The chain of transmission is documented in personnel as well as law. After the Irish War of Independence, roughly 650 former members of the Black and Tans paramilitary force were redeployed to form the British Palestine Gendarmerie, bringing methods of violent interrogation with them. Albanese noted that Britain’s Mandate-era legal framework, including administrative detention without charge, was incorporated wholesale into Israeli law.</p>
<p>Former Addameer director Abdel Latif Ghaith recalled witnessing Rasmea Odeh being tortured in a Jerusalem detention centre in 1969, where he saw her naked and her father forced into the room. Odeh later told a UN committee that she was raped with a stick and subjected to electric shocks to her mouth and genitals.</p>
<p>The documentary further shows that Israel later adopted techniques refined by France during the Algerian war. Former International Criminal Court judge Cuno Tarfusser stated that “sexual crimes become a method to fight the war.” Kifaya Khraim, international advocacy coordinator at the Women’s Centre for Legal Aid and Counselling, said that Israeli forces exploit the social stigma around sexual violence. “Israeli forces know of it, and they use it and they exploit it,” she told Al Jazeera.</p>
