<p>UN special rapporteur Francesca Albanese has detailed the colonial roots of sexual torture employed by Israeli forces against Palestinian detainees, describing it as an institutional practice directly inherited from British counterinsurgency methods and maintained throughout Israel’s history. The Al Jazeera investigation also documents how France’s torture doctrine in Algeria further shaped Israeli practice.</p><p>In an Al Jazeera investigation published on Thursday, Albanese said torture had been a hallmark of Israel’s treatment of Palestinians “since the very beginning of its existence.” She traced the lineage to British paramilitaries known as the Black and Tans, who were deployed to Mandate Palestine in 1922 after brutalising Ireland and formed up to 95 percent of the Palestine Gendarmerie. The Mandate’s emergency regulations, including indefinite administrative detention, were immediately absorbed into Israel’s legal system and remain in force.</p><p>The report documents how France’s torture doctrine in Algeria during the 1950s and 1960s further shaped Israeli practice. Survivors, including 1969 detainee Rasmea Odeh, described rape with sticks, electric shocks to genitals and sexual humiliation staged in front of family members. Kifaya Khraim of the Ramallah-based Women’s Centre for Legal Aid and Counselling said Israeli forces exploit the social stigma around sexual violence, noting that many women tell groups like hers of devices inserted inside them “without being able to identify that as rape or sexual violence.”</p><p>Former International Criminal Court judge Cuno Tarfusser said sexual crimes become a method to fight the war, and under international law such sexual violence can be prosecuted as a war crime and crime against humanity. Albanese added that the infliction of humiliation and erasure was “a practice to control” in colonial systems. Advocates warn the colonial counterinsurgency playbook remains substantially intact, raising direct accountability questions for legal systems across the Global South.</p>
