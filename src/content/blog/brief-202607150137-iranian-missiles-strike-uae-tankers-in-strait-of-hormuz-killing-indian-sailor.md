The United Arab Emirates Ministry of Defence condemned a "blatant attack" after Iranian cruise missiles struck two Emirati oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz on July 14, 2026, killing one Indian crew member and injuring eight others, including six Indians and two Ukrainians. The ministry said the tankers, Mombasa and Al Bahiyah, were targeted while in Omani territorial waters, causing material damage after fires broke on board.

The attack comes amid heightened tensions since February when the United States and Israel launched attacks on Iran, threatening global energy security as 20% of the world's oil and gas traffic passes through the strategic waterway. UAE Defence Ministry officials said the country retained "its full right to respond" and remained fully prepared to deal with any threats to its security and stability.

Separately, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency reported a tanker hit by an unknown projectile 40 nautical miles northeast of Oman's Qalhat, though Reuters could not verify if it referred to the same incident. Iran has not commented on the attacks, while President Donald Trump warned U.S. forces would hit Iran again "very hard" after the two sides exchanged missile and drone attacks.
