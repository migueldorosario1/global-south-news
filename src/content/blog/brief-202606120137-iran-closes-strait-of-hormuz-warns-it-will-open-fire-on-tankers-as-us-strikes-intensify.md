<p>Iran’s military announced on Thursday a complete closure of the Strait of Hormuz to all oil tankers and commercial ships, warning that any vessel attempting passage would be fired upon. The declaration marked an immediate escalation in the tit-for-tat cycle of attacks following United States strikes this week on the Iranian port city of Bandar Abbas and nearby Qeshm Island.</p>
<p>US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth confirmed on Wednesday that Washington had launched new strikes on key facilities in Iran. Earlier, CENTCOM described the operations as self-defense and a proportional response to attacks on US forces and international shipping. President Donald Trump stated that Iran had taken too long to negotiate a deal and now they will have to ‘pay the price’.</p>
<p>The strategic waterway, through which roughly 20 percent of global oil and liquefied natural gas normally transits, has been subject to sporadic closure since US-Israeli strikes on Tehran in late February. While a fragile ceasefire was brokered in April, Iran’s latest announcement risks further deepening volatility in energy markets already rattled by the months-long confrontation.</p>
<p>For developing economies in the Global South, the renewed disruption threatens to amplify fuel import costs and exacerbate energy poverty. The strait’s status as the sole maritime exit for major Gulf producers means that even restricted passage can send crude prices climbing well above pre-crisis levels, straining the fiscal positions of net-energy-importing nations across Asia, Africa and Latin America.</p>
<p>Analysts warned of the danger of miscalculation. Elijah Magnier, a Brussels-based military analyst, told Al Jazeera that each side believes it can control the escalation, but a single strike crossing a red line could erode restraint and widen the conflict. Iran has previously allowed selective passage to some ships, yet Thursday’s blanket interdiction order eliminates that ambiguity, at least until diplomatic channels can reassert control over the strait.</p>
