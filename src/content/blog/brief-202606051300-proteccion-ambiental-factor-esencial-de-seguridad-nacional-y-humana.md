<p>Vietnamese President To Lam has declared that environmental protection is a fundamental pillar of national and human security, warning that economic growth cannot be considered sustainable if citizens live amid pollution and environmental degradation. “A country may record high economic growth, but if its citizens live in conditions of pollution and environmental degradation, it cannot be called truly sustainable development,” Lam wrote in an article published to mark World Environment Day and World Oceans Day.</p>
<p>In the article, titled “For an ecological civilisation, a green Vietnam and a peaceful and sustainable ocean,” Lam called for deeper reflection on humanity’s relationship with nature. He argued that the world is witnessing unprecedented ecological changes that are pushing natural limits to dangerous levels, posing a fundamental question: can development be sustainable if the ecological basis of life is being eroded?</p>
<p>Lam stressed that a safe environment and peaceful oceans are essential for development, security, peace, justice, and survival. “The oceans further demonstrate the interdependence of humanity,” he wrote, adding that protecting them is not just an environmental obligation but a requirement for peace, cooperation, and shared international responsibility.</p>
<p>Vietnam, a maritime nation with over 3,260 kilometres of coastline, two major deltas, and numerous coastal cities, is severely affected by climate change. Lam highlighted the country’s commitment to achieving net zero emissions by 2050, promoting a just energy transition, reducing ocean plastic waste, and developing a circular economy. He insisted these commitments are “not imposed from outside, but stem from the country’s inherent needs.”</p>
<p>The president called on developed nations to fully deliver on climate financing pledges, technology transfer, human resource training, and market access for green products from developing countries. He urged developing countries to transform growth models, use support effectively, and strengthen transparency and accountability in meeting environmental commitments.</p>
