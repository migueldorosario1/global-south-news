<p>An Indian sailor was killed and eight other crew members wounded when Iranian cruise missiles struck two oil tankers flying the United Arab Emirates flag in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, according to the UAE Ministry of Defence.</p>
<p>The ministry said the vessels, the Mombasa and Al Bahiyah, were hit while travelling through the southern lane of the waterway, inside Omani territorial waters. The deceased was a crew member aboard the Mombasa. Among the eight wounded, four remained in serious condition; six of the injured were Indian nationals and two were Ukrainian.</p>
<p>Fires erupted on both tankers following the strike but were later brought under control, the ministry statement added. It condemned the incident as a “blatant attack” and asserted that the UAE retained “its full right to respond to this escalation”.</p>
<p>The attack deepens the crisis that has gripped the region since the United States and Israel launched military strikes on Iran in late February. President Donald Trump said on Monday that Washington was reinstating its naval blockade of Iranian shipping and warned of further force to keep the Strait of Hormuz open. Iran’s joint military command countered that the US had no role in determining the waterway’s future and would not be permitted to intervene.</p>
<p>Before the conflict, roughly a fifth of global oil and gas transited the Strait of Hormuz daily, moving more than 15 million barrels to world markets. The targeting of commercial vessels in Omani waters heightens risks for seafarers from labour-exporting nations such as India and threatens the stability of a chokepoint critical to energy security across the Global South.</p>
