<p>Uruguay’s Center for Molecular Imaging (Cudim) has received ethical approval to start human trials of a new radiopharmaceutical that could improve the early diagnosis of Alzheimer’s disease, the presidency announced. The decision allows the Montevideo-based centre to move a promising tracer from preclinical success to clinical testing.</p><p>Alarico Rodríguez, Cudim’s director general, said the Alzheimer’s project is being developed in close collaboration with the Faculty of Medicine, the Hospital de Clínicas and the Academic Geriatrics Unit. The radiopharmaceutical, intended for the diagnosis of neurodegenerative pathologies, successfully completed its preclinical phase and will now enter human trials.</p><p>The initiative is one of 40 research projects under way at Cudim, where 350 professionals focus on early detection of complex diseases. According to the presidency, 27 studies are in full execution, while the other 13 are undergoing review by institutional ethics panels and national and international funding agencies.</p><p>Founded in 2011, Cudim has forged a strong reputation in oncology, developing novel PET tracers for prostate cancer and other tumours. It is the only institution in Latin America to operate animal imaging equipment with capabilities comparable to human-use technology, providing a unique translational research platform. The presidency also noted the prominent role of women scientists at the centre.</p>
