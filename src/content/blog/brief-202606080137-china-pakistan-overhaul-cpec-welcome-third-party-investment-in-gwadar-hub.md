<p>China and Pakistan have struck a "new broad consensus" to overhaul their flagship economic corridor and turn the Arabian Sea port of Gwadar into a regional connectivity hub, with both sides also welcoming third-party participation in the corridor's development, the two governments announced on Tuesday as Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif wrapped up a visit to Beijing.</p>
<p>During talks with President Xi Jinping and Premier Li Qiang, Sharif committed to accelerating high-quality development of the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor, a signature project of Beijing’s Belt and Road Initiative. The plans include upgrading the Karakoram Highway and the Khunjerab Pass — the main overland artery between the two countries — and strengthening road and port links. Pakistan also pledged targeted security steps to safeguard Chinese personnel and investments, addressing a pressing concern for Beijing after repeated militant attacks on its nationals and projects.</p>
<p>The joint statement explicitly welcomed third-party participation in CPEC under an agreed model, signalling that Islamabad and Beijing aim to broaden the corridor’s funding base and integrate it more closely with regional trade. With Gwadar envisioned as a node for transhipment and energy corridors, the upgraded infrastructure is expected to deepen connections between South and Central Asia.</p>
<p>The diplomatic dimension added weight to the infrastructure push. China praised Pakistan's efforts in easing a temporary U.S.-Iran ceasefire and hosting talks in Islamabad; both sides reiterated support for a five-point initiative to restore stability in the region. Pakistan reaffirmed its one-China commitment, describing Taiwan as an "inalienable" part of China and opposing any form of independence. The neighbours also vowed to prevent groups such as the Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan and the Eastern Turkistan Islamic Movement from using their territories to threaten regional security.</p>
<p>The accord provides a renewed political mandate for a corridor that has faced delays and security headwinds, while offering a potential template for Global South infrastructure partnerships.</p>
