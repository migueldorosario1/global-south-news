<p>The United Arab Emirates Ministry of Defence said on Tuesday that Iranian cruise missiles struck two Emirati oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, killing one Indian crew member and wounding eight others in the latest escalation to threaten global energy supply routes. The ministry identified the vessels as the Mombasa and Al Bahiyah and said they were hit while transiting the southern lane of the strategic waterway inside Omani territorial waters.</p>

<p>The dead crew member was aboard the Mombasa, the ministry said. Among the eight wounded, six were Indian nationals and two were Ukrainian nationals; four of the injured were listed in serious condition. The attack caused fires on both tankers, which the ministry said had since been brought under control. It condemned the strike as a “blatant attack” and stated that the UAE “retains its full right to respond to this escalation”.</p>

<p>The incident underscores the deteriorating security situation in the Gulf since February 28, when the United States and Israel launched military operations against Iran. The Strait of Hormuz normally carries about a fifth of the world’s daily oil and gas traffic, moving more than 15 million barrels of fuel worth at least $1.2 billion. The attack triggered warnings from maritime security bodies. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency reported that a tanker was hit by an unknown projectile 40 nautical miles northeast of Oman’s Qalhat, though it was not immediately clear if this referred to the same incident.</p>

<p>President Donald Trump said on Monday the US was reinstating its blockade of Iranian shipping in the Gulf and would ensure the strait remains open, while warning of further strikes on Iran. Tehran’s joint military command rejected any US role in the waterway’s future. The latest violence has cast doubt over an interim US-Iranian agreement signed last month to halt hostilities and reopen the strait. Iran has not yet commented on the attack.</p>
