<p>The Strait of Hormuz, which normally carries about 20 percent of global oil and liquefied natural gas transits, has been under tight Iranian control since late February. Tehran had at times selectively allowed some vessels to pass, reportedly charging tolls as high as $2m per ship. Its latest order, however, returns the waterway to a total blockade. For import-dependent developing countries in the Global South, the closure threatens to drive energy prices even higher and deepen economic instability, with few short-term alternatives to the threatened supply route.</p>
