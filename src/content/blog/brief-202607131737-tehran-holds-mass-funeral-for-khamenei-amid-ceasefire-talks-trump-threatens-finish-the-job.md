<p>A vast sea of black-clad mourners filled Tehran’s streets on Monday as Iran’s theocracy orchestrated a mass funeral procession for Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, who was killed more than four months ago in the Israeli-US airstrike that ignited the West Asia war. The flag-draped coffin of the 86-year-old cleric, along with those of his family members slain in the February 28 attack, was borne through the capital on a truck decorated to resemble the ornamental grating of an imam’s shrine, bound for Mehrabad International Airport. Chants of “Death to Trump” rang out repeatedly from the crowd, aimed at US President Donald Trump.</p>
<p>The staged spectacle, intended by the ruling establishment to project domestic cohesion, unfolded while Iran and the United States are locked in negotiations for a permanent ceasefire. Trump reinforced the precarious state of those talks by declaring that a deal would be struck or Washington would “finish the job.” Revolutionary Guard General Hasan Hasanzsdeh, who oversaw the 12-hour procession, described the route to the airport as a meticulously planned tribute linking Khamenei’s legacy to the country’s revolutionary symbolism.</p>
<p>The funeral came more than four months after the airstrike that killed Khamenei, a delay that underscored the depth of disruption caused by a conflict that has since drawn in multiple state and non-state actors. For the Global South, the ongoing war has compounded energy security risks, deepened regional instability, and curbed the diplomatic space available to non-aligned nations. As the ceremony unfolded, governments from New Delhi to Brasília grappled with fallout that has pushed oil price volatility to new highs and tested their ability to mediate between the principal belligerents without sacrificing positions of principled neutrality.</p>
