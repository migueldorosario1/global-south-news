<p>Iranian cruise missiles struck two UAE-flagged oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, killing one Indian crew member and wounding eight others, the United Arab Emirates Ministry of Defence said. The attack, which hit the Mombasa and Al Bahiyah while they were in Omani territorial waters, marks a dangerous escalation in the strategic waterway amid spiralling US-Iran hostilities.</p>

<p>The ministry said the dead crew member was aboard the Mombasa. Among the injured, four were in a serious condition; six were Indian nationals and two were Ukrainian. Fires broke out on both vessels after the projectiles struck but have since been brought under control. In a statement, the ministry condemned what it called a “blatant attack” and said the UAE retained “its full right to respond to this escalation”. It added that the country was taking all necessary measures to respond firmly to any threats to its security.</p>

<p>The incident follows weeks of heightened tensions since the United States and Israel launched attacks on Iran on February 28. On Monday, President Donald Trump announced the reimposition of a US blockade on Iranian shipping in the Gulf and warned of further strikes, while asserting that the strait would remain open for a fee. Iran’s military command said Washington had no role in determining the waterway’s future.</p>

<p>The Strait of Hormuz normally handles about a fifth of the world’s oil and gas traffic, delivering more than 15 million barrels of fuel daily. For Global South nations heavily reliant on these energy supplies and whose seafarers often crew commercial vessels, the attack underscores the acute risks posed by the conflict—both to economic stability and to the lives of maritime workers caught in the crossfire. The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency separately reported a projectile hitting a tanker’s engine room in the same area, though it was not immediately clear if the report referred to the same event. Iran has not commented on the strikes.</p>
