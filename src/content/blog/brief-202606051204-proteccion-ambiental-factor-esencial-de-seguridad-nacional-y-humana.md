<p>Environmental protection is a fundamental pillar of national and human security, Vietnamese President To Lam has argued in an essay marking World Environment Day and World Oceans Day. Lam, who also serves as General Secretary of the Communist Party of Vietnam, warned that high economic growth is meaningless if citizens live amid pollution and environmental degradation, making truly sustainable development impossible.</p>
<p>In the article titled “For an Ecological Civilisation, a Green Vietnam and a Peaceful, Sustainable Ocean,” Lam stressed that humanity’s relationship with nature is undergoing profound transformations that demand new approaches to development, responsibility and action. He cited “unprecedented deep ecological changes” affecting every continent and cautioned that nature’s limits are being pushed to dangerous extremes, eroding the ecological base that sustains life.</p>
<p>A modern society, Lam wrote, generates wealth within ecological boundaries, uses resources responsibly and treats nature as a condition for existence, a national asset and a legacy for future generations. He added that oceans expose humanity’s interdependence, making their protection not just an environmental duty but a prerequisite for peace, cooperation and respect for international law.</p>
<p>Vietnam, a maritime nation with more than 3,260 kilometres of coastline and two major deltas that is severely affected by climate change, has shown a strong sense of responsibility, Lam noted. The country is committed to net-zero emissions by 2050, a just energy transition, reduced marine plastic waste, a circular economy and biodiversity conservation. He insisted these commitments arise from Vietnam’s own inherent needs, not from external obligations.</p>
<p>Lam called on developed countries to fully deliver on pledges for climate finance, technology transfer, human-resource training, governance capacity-building and expanded market access for green products from developing nations. Developing countries, he added, must show the determination to transform their growth models, use support effectively and strengthen transparency and accountability in meeting their environmental commitments.</p>
