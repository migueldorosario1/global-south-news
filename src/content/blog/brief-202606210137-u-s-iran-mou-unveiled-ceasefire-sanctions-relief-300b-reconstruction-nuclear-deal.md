<p>The United States on June 17 released the full text of a memorandum of understanding with Iran, outlining an immediate and permanent halt to military operations on all fronts, the lifting of Washington’s naval blockade within 30 days, and a $300 billion reconstruction fund for Iran. The document, made public by the U.S. government, establishes a framework for negotiations toward a final agreement within 60 days, extendable by mutual consent.</p>
<p>Under the MOU, Iran pledges safe passage for commercial vessels through the Strait of Hormuz at no charge for two months, while both sides commit to negotiating the complete termination of sanctions against Iran—including UN Security Council and IAEA board resolutions and all unilateral US sanctions—in tandem with a schedule for Iran to down-blend its stockpiled enriched uranium under IAEA supervision. Pending a final deal, Washington will issue waivers for Iranian crude exports, unlock frozen assets, and refrain from new sanctions or additional troop deployments; Iran will maintain the current status quo of its nuclear programme.</p>
<p>An executive mechanism will monitor compliance, and the final accord is to be endorsed by a binding UN Security Council resolution. Negotiations on outstanding details, such as the precise sequencing of sanctions relief and the disposition of nuclear material, are to start once the ceasefire, naval blockade removal, safe-passage, oil-export waivers, and asset-release provisions take effect. For the Global South, the deal could stabilise critical oil supply chains and temper energy price premiums, opening avenues for investment in Iran’s reconstruction. Enforcement and verification remain uncertain, however, and the 60-day deadline to translate pledges into a binding pact will test the durability of the breakthrough.</p>
