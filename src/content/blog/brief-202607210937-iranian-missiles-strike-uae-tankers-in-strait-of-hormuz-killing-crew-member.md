<p>The United Arab Emirates’ Ministry of Defence said on Tuesday that Iranian cruise missiles struck two of its oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, killing one Indian crew member and wounding eight others. The ministry condemned the “blatant attack” and said the UAE retained “its full right to respond to this escalation.”</p>
<p>The tankers, the Mombasa and Al Bahiyah, were targeted in the southern lane of the strait while in Omani territorial waters, the ministry said. The deceased crew member was aboard the Mombasa. Six of the wounded were Indian nationals and two were Ukrainian, with four in serious condition. Fires broke out on both vessels but were later brought under control, it added.</p>
<p>The strike intensifies hostilities in a waterway that before the conflict handled roughly 20 per cent of the world’s daily oil and gas traffic. Tensions have surged since February 28, when the US and Israel launched military operations against Iran. On Monday, President Donald Trump said Washington was reinstating its blockade of Iranian shipping and warned of further strikes, while Iran’s military rejected any US role in the strait’s future.</p>
<p>No immediate comment was available from Iranian authorities. The violence has cast doubt on an interim US-Iranian agreement signed last month to halt hostilities and reopen the strategic passage.</p>
