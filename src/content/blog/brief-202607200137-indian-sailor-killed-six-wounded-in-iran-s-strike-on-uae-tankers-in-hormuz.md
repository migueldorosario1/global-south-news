<p>Iranian cruise missiles struck two Emirati oil tankers in Omani territorial waters of the Strait of Hormuz on July 14, killing one Indian crew member and wounding eight others, the United Arab Emirates Ministry of Defence said on Tuesday. The attack on the tankers Mombasa and Al Bahiyah marks another flashpoint in a waterway that has become a central theatre of the months-long US-Iran conflict.</p>
<p>The ministry said the vessels were targeted while transiting the southern lane of the strait. The deceased seafarer was aboard the Mombasa. Six of the wounded were Indian nationals and two were Ukrainian, according to the ministry. Fires ignited on both tankers but were brought under control.</p>
<p>The UAE condemned what it called a “blatant attack” and said it retained “its full right to respond to this escalation,” adding that it was prepared to take all necessary measures against threats to its security and stability. Iran has not commented on the strikes.</p>
<p>The incident deepens risks for the millions of Indian migrant workers in Gulf states and threatens a maritime chokepoint that moves around a fifth of the world’s daily oil and gas supply. Developing economies that depend on affordable energy imports are especially exposed to the disruption; before the conflict erupted in February, more than 15 million barrels of fuel passed through Hormuz each day.</p>
<p>Hostilities have escalated since the United States and Israel launched attacks on Iran on February 28. On Monday, President Donald Trump announced that Washington was reinstating its blockade of Iranian shipping and would keep the strait open “for a fee,” while warning of fresh strikes. Iran’s top joint military command dismissed any US role in the waterway’s future. The crisis has already destabilised the Gulf region, and a tentative ceasefire and strait-reopening agreement signed last month now hangs in the balance.</p>
