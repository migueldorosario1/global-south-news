<p>The United Arab Emirates said on Tuesday that Iranian cruise missiles hit two of its oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, killing an Indian crew member and wounding eight others, including six Indian nationals and two Ukrainians.</p>

<p>The UAE Ministry of Defence identified the vessels as the Mombasa and Al Bahiyah, and said they were struck in the southern lane of the strait while in Omani territorial waters. The dead seafarer was aboard the Mombasa. Four of the wounded were in a serious condition, the ministry said, adding that fires on both ships had been brought under control.</p>

<p>The ministry condemned what it called a “blatant attack” and said the UAE retained “its full right to respond to this escalation”. It added that the country was taking all necessary measures to respond firmly to any threats.</p>

<p>Separately, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency reported that a tanker was hit by an unknown projectile 40 nautical miles northeast of Oman’s Qalhat on Tuesday. The master said the projectile struck the starboard-side engine room but all crew were safe. Reuters could not immediately verify whether the UKMTO report referred to the same incident. Iran has not commented on the attacks.</p>

<p>The strikes mark a sharp escalation in a conflict that erupted on February 28 with US and Israeli attacks on Iran. On Monday, US President Donald Trump said Washington was reinstating a blockade of Iranian shipping in the Gulf and would ensure the strait remained open “for a fee”, while warning of further military action. Iran’s top joint military command said the US had no role in determining the waterway’s future. Before the conflict, about a fifth of global oil and gas traffic transited Hormuz daily.</p>
