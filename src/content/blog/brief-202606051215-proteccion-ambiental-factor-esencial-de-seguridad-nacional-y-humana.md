<p>Vietnamese leader To Lam declared environmental protection a fundamental factor of national and human security in an article marking World Environment Day and World Oceans Day. He warned that even high economic growth cannot count as truly sustainable development if citizens live in conditions of pollution and environmental degradation.</p><p>Writing under the title “For an ecological civilisation, a green Vietnam and a peaceful and sustainable ocean,” Lam argued that humanity’s relationship with nature is facing unprecedented transformations that demand fresh approaches to development, responsibility and action. A secure environment and sustainable oceans, he said, are central to the survival, peace and justice of nations.</p><p>Lam pointed to deep ecological changes affecting every continent, stressing that “nature’s limits are being pushed to dangerous levels.” He called for societies to generate wealth within ecological boundaries, treat resources responsibly and view nature as an indispensable national heritage and a legacy for future generations. The oceans, he added, highlight humanity’s interdependence and make their protection an obligation for peace, international law and shared responsibility.</p><p>Vietnam’s own vulnerability as a maritime nation with more than 3,260 kilometres of coastline, two major deltas and millions of coastal residents gives these questions acute local relevance, Lam noted. He reaffirmed the country’s commitment to achieving net-zero emissions by 2050, driving a just energy transition, curbing plastic waste and building a circular economy, describing these steps as inherent national needs rather than externally imposed demands.</p><p>The article urged developed countries to deliver fully on climate finance pledges, technology transfer and governance support. Developing nations, Lam stressed, must simultaneously transform their growth models, use available support effectively and strengthen transparency and accountability in their own environmental commitments. The future of global security, he concluded, rests on collective ecological stewardship.</p>
