<p>Iranian cruise missiles struck two UAE-flagged oil tankers in Omani territorial waters in the Strait of Hormuz on July 14, killing one Indian crew member and wounding eight others, the United Arab Emirates Ministry of Defence said. The tankers, the Mombasa and Al Bahiyah, were targeted in the southern lane of the strait, the ministry stated. The dead sailor was aboard the Mombasa, while six of the wounded were Indian nationals and two were Ukrainian.</p>

<p>Fires broke out on both vessels but were later brought under control. The ministry condemned the “blatant attack” and said the UAE retained “its full right to respond to this escalation”, adding that it remained prepared to deal with any threats.</p>

<p>Iran did not immediately comment on the attack. A separate advisory from the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations reported that a tanker was hit by an unknown projectile 40 nautical miles northeast of Oman’s Qalhat, striking the starboard-side engine room, though all crew were safe. It was unclear if the two incidents were linked.</p>

<p>The strike escalates weeks of volatility since the US and Israel launched a military offensive against Iran on February 28. On July 13, US President Donald Trump announced the reimposition of a blockade on Iranian shipping and threatened further heavy strikes, while insisting Washington would keep the strait open “for a fee”. Iran’s top military command retorted that the US had no role in determining the waterway’s future.</p>

<p>The Strait of Hormuz is a vital global energy passage that carried more than 15 million barrels of oil daily before the conflict. The latest attack directly threatens Global South nations reliant on secure transit routes and exposes their nationals to collateral harm, as the casualties among Indian seafarers illustrate.</p>
