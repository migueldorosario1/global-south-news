<p>An Indian crew member was killed and eight other sailors wounded when Iranian cruise missiles struck two Emirati oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, the United Arab Emirates Ministry of Defence said, sharply escalating tensions in the strategic waterway.</p>
<p>The ministry said the tankers Mombasa and Al Bahiyah were hit in the southern lane of the strait while transiting Omani territorial waters. The fatality occurred aboard the Mombasa, while six Indians and two Ukrainians were among the injured, four of them seriously. Fires broke out on both vessels but were later brought under control.</p>
<p>The UAE condemned the strike as a “blatant attack” and said it retained “its full right to respond to this escalation”, adding it was ready to deal with any threats and would take necessary measures to safeguard national security and stability. Iran has not commented on the incident.</p>
<p>The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency separately reported that a tanker was hit by an unknown projectile 40 nautical miles northeast of Oman’s Qalhat, puncturing the starboard-side engine room. All crew on that vessel were reported safe. It was not immediately clear whether the UKMTO report referred to the same attack.</p>
<p>The strikes follow weeks of soaring hostility in the Gulf since the United States and Israel launched attacks on Iran on February 28. On Monday, President Donald Trump said Washington was reinstating a blockade of Iranian shipping and would ensure the strait remained open “for a fee”, warning U.S. forces would hit Iran again “very hard”. Iran’s military command said the U.S. had no role in determining the waterway’s future and would not be allowed to intervene.</p>
<p>The violence has thrown into doubt an interim agreement signed last month intended to reopen the strait and halt hostilities. Before the conflict, roughly a fifth of the world’s daily oil and gas traffic — more than 15 million barrels worth at least $1.2 billion — passed through Hormuz.</p>
