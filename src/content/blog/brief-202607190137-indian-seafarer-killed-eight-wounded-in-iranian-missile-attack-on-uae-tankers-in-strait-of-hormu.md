<p>An Indian seafarer was killed and eight other crew members wounded after Iranian cruise missiles struck two Emirati oil tankers in Omani waters of the Strait of Hormuz, the United Arab Emirates Ministry of Defence said on Tuesday (July 14, 2026). The attack on the tankers Mombasa and Al Bahiyah marks the latest escalation in a conflict that has thrown the strategic waterway into turmoil.</p>
<p>The ministry said the vessels were traveling in the southern lane of the strait when they came under missile fire. The fatal casualty occurred aboard the Mombasa. Of the eight wounded—six Indian nationals and two Ukrainians—four suffered serious injuries, the ministry added. Fires broke out on both tankers but were later brought under control.</p>
<p>The UAE condemned what it called a “blatant attack” and said it retained “its full right to respond to this escalation.” It added that the country was taking all necessary measures to respond firmly to attempts to undermine its security.</p>
<p>The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency separately reported that a tanker was hit by an unknown projectile about 40 nautical miles northeast of Oman’s Qalhat. The projectile struck the starboard-side engine room, but the crew were reported safe. It was not immediately clear whether the UKMTO report referred to the same incident.</p>
<p>The assault follows weeks of heightened tensions after the United States and Israel launched strikes on Iran on February 28, opening a new phase of hostilities. President Donald Trump said on Monday that the U.S. was reinstating a blockade of Iranian shipping in the Gulf and warned of further military action. Iran’s top military command responded that the U.S. had no role in deciding the waterway’s future.</p>
<p>Before the conflict erupted, roughly a fifth of global oil and gas traffic passed through the Strait of Hormuz daily, pumping more than 15 million barrels of fuel worth at least $1.2 billion into world markets. The attack on commercial shipping in Omani waters threatens to deepen the disruption, with direct consequences for the many developing-nation seafarers who crew vessels on the route.</p>
