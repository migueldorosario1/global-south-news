<p>UN Special Rapporteur Francesca Albanese has traced the Israeli security apparatus’s systematic use of sexual violence against Palestinian detainees to a colonial lineage directly inherited from British and French counterinsurgency doctrines. In a companion essay to Al Jazeera’s film <em>Bodies of Evidence</em>, Albanese states that British emergency regulations crafted for Mandate Palestine were “immediately received and ingrained in the Israeli system,” replicating techniques first deployed against the Irish independence movement and later refined by France in Algeria.</p>
<p>Former detainee and Addameer director Abdel Latif Ghaith recounts witnessing Rasmea Odeh naked in a Jerusalem interrogation room in 1969, and seeing her father brought in. After the father left, Ghaith says Odeh was later severely tortured. Her testimony before a UN committee, recorded years before the Convention Against Torture was adopted, described rape with a stick, electric shocks to her mouth and genitals, and threats that her father would be forced to rape her. Ghaith insists she was not the first such victim.</p>
<p>Kifaya Khraim, international advocacy coordinator at the Ramallah-based Women’s Centre for Legal Aid and Counselling, says Israeli forces exploit the social stigma around sexual violence, with many women describing devices inserted inside them without recognizing it as rape.</p>
<p>Cuno Tarfusser, a former judge at the International Criminal Court, argues that in such contexts sexual violence ceases to be an incidental crime and becomes “a method to fight the war.” The Al Jazeera investigation traces how British forces redeployed veterans of the Black and Tans paramilitary units to form the Palestine Gendarmerie in 1922, bringing their methods from the Irish conflict. The source highlights a chain of sexual violence inherited from British counterinsurgency in Ireland, through French practices in Algeria, to Israeli detention methods.</p>
