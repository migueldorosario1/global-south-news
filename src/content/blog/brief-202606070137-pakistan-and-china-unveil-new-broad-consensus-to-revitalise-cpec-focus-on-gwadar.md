<p>Pakistan and China unveiled a sweeping "new broad consensus" on Tuesday to revitalise the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor and position the Arabian Sea port of Gwadar as a hub of regional connectivity. The agreement came as Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif met President Xi Jinping and Premier Li Qiang during a visit to Beijing aimed at securing investment and reinforcing strategic ties.</p>
<p>The two sides committed to "high-quality" development of CPEC, the flagship of China's Belt and Road Initiative, and said they would upgrade the Karakoram Highway and the Khunjerab Pass, the main overland artery linking the countries. In a joint statement they explicitly welcomed third-party participation in the corridor under a mutually agreed model. Pakistan also pledged targeted measures to boost security for Chinese personnel and investments, a persistent concern for Beijing after repeated militant attacks on its nationals and projects.</p>
<p>China praised Islamabad’s mediation efforts in easing the temporary U.S.-Iran ceasefire, and both governments reiterated support for early adoption of a five-point initiative to restore peace in West Asia. On Afghanistan, they jointly opposed the use of territory by militant groups, including the Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan and the Eastern Turkistan Islamic Movement, to threaten regional security.</p>
<p>Sharif reaffirmed Pakistan’s commitment to the one-China principle, calling Taiwan an "inalienable" part of China and opposing any form of independence.</p>
