<p>DUBAI — Iranian cruise missiles struck two Emirati oil tankers in the southern lane of the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, killing one Indian crew member and wounding eight others, the United Arab Emirates Ministry of Defence said. The attack hit the vessels while they were in Omani territorial waters, intensifying risks for the Global South’s energy lifeline and its maritime workforce.</p>
<p>The ministry named the tankers as the Mombasa and Al Bahiyah. The fatality occurred aboard the Mombasa, and four of the wounded were in serious condition. Six Indian nationals and two Ukrainians were among the injured, it said, confirming that fires triggered by the strike on both ships had been brought under control.</p>
<p>The UAE condemned the incident as a “blatant attack” and stated it retained “its full right to respond to this escalation.” The ministry added that the country was taking all necessary measures to deal with threats to its security. Iran has not yet commented on the assault.</p>
<p>Tuesday’s violence escalates a months-long crisis that began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran in late February. It comes a day after US President Donald Trump announced the reimposition of a naval blockade on Iranian shipping in the Gulf, warning of further strikes, while Tehran insisted that Washington had no role in policing the waterway. The Strait of Hormuz normally handles about a fifth of global oil and gas flows, and its disruption directly threatens fuel supplies and prices across emerging economies, as well as the safety of thousands of Indian, Filipino and other Global South seafarers who transit these routes.</p>
<p>Separately, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency reported that a tanker was hit by an unknown projectile 40 nautical miles northeast of Oman’s Qalhat, with the projectile striking the engine room but causing no casualties. It was unclear if that report referred to the same incident.</p>
