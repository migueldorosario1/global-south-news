<p>China and Pakistan announced a broad new consensus on May 26, 2026 to accelerate the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) and transform the Gwadar deep-water port into a regional connectivity hub, following high-level meetings in Beijing.</p>

<p>Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif held talks with President Xi Jinping and Premier Li Qiang, after which a joint statement laid out plans for “high-quality” CPEC development. Both governments welcomed third-party participation in corridor projects “under the model agreed” and committed to upgrading the Karakoram Highway; the plans also encompass the Khunjerab Pass, the high-altitude border crossing that serves as the primary overland link between the two countries.</p>

<p>In turn, Pakistan pledged targeted security measures to protect Chinese personnel and investments, a key demand of Beijing after years of militant attacks.</p>

<p>The agreement was reached at a time when Islamabad is navigating border tensions with Afghanistan and mediating in the Iran war. China expressed appreciation for Islamabad’s diplomatic efforts, including its role in easing a temporary U.S.-Iran ceasefire and hosting talks. Both sides voiced support for a five-point peace plan for West Asia.</p>

<p>Pakistan reiterated the one-China principle, declaring Taiwan an “inalienable” part of China and opposing any form of Taiwanese independence. The two countries also jointly opposed the use of territory by militant groups—including the Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan and Eastern Turkistan Islamic Movement—to threaten regional security or launch attacks.</p>
