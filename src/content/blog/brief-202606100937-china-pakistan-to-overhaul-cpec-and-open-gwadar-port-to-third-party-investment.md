<p>Pakistan and China agreed to overhaul their flagship economic corridor and open the strategic port of Gwadar to third-party investment, as Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif concluded a visit to Beijing on May 26. A joint statement issued after Sharif met President Xi Jinping and Premier Li Qiang said the two sides had reached a “new broad consensus” on deepening ties and transforming Gwadar into a regional connectivity hub.</p>

<p>The leaders committed to advancing high-quality development of the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor, a central project of Beijing’s Belt and Road Initiative. Plans include upgrading the Karakoram Highway, the main overland link between the neighbours, and enhancing facilities at the Khunjerab Pass. Crucially, the statement said both countries “welcomed third parties to participate in the development of CPEC under the model agreed,” signalling an intent to broaden the corridor’s financing and reach.</p>

<p>Sharif’s government pledged targeted measures to improve security for Chinese nationals and investments in Pakistan, a persistent concern for Beijing following repeated militant attacks on its projects. The promise underscores Islamabad’s effort to maintain investor confidence amid domestic instability.</p>

<p>The visit also highlighted Pakistan’s growing regional diplomatic role. China expressed appreciation for Islamabad’s mediation in securing a temporary ceasefire between the US and Iran, and both sides reiterated support for a five-point initiative to restore peace in West Asia. On Afghanistan, Pakistan welcomed Chinese efforts to facilitate dialogue, while the two countries jointly opposed the use of territory by militant groups, including the Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan and the Eastern Turkistan Islamic Movement, to threaten regional security.</p>

<p>Pakistan reaffirmed its adherence to the one-China principle, calling Taiwan an “inalienable” part of China and opposing any form of independence. The talks signal Beijing’s intent to deepen a strategic partnership that increasingly intertwines economic ambition with complex regional security dynamics, testing the corridor’s viability as a vehicle for South-South cooperation.</p>
