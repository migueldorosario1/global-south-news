<p>An Indian crew member was killed and eight others wounded when Iranian cruise missiles struck two Emirati oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, the United Arab Emirates Ministry of Defence said. The vessels, the Mombasa and Al Bahiyah, were hit while navigating the southern lane of the waterway in Omani territorial waters.</p>
<p>The ministry identified the dead crew member as a national of India and confirmed that six of the eight wounded were also Indian, with the remaining two from Ukraine. Four of the injured were described as being in serious condition. Fires that broke out on both tankers after the attack were brought under control, the ministry added.</p>
<p>Condemning the strikes as a “blatant attack”, the UAE said it retains “its full right to respond to this escalation” and remains prepared to deal with any threats to its security and stability. Separately, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency reported that an unknown projectile hit a tanker’s starboard-side engine room about 40 nautical miles northeast of Oman’s Qalhat, though it was not immediately clear if that related to the same incident.</p>
<p>The assault intensifies an already volatile security environment in the Gulf. The United States and Israel launched military strikes on Iran on February 28, triggering a conflict that has repeatedly disrupted maritime traffic. On Monday, U.S. President Donald Trump said Washington was reinstating a blockade of Iranian shipping and warned of further strikes, while asserting that the strait would stay open “for a fee”. Iran’s joint military command responded that the U.S. has no role in determining the waterway’s future.</p>
<p>Before the war, roughly a fifth of the world’s oil and gas — more than 15 million barrels daily, worth at least $1.2 billion — transited the Strait of Hormuz. The attack directly exposes the lethal risks for migrant maritime workers and threatens the energy supply chains on which many developing economies depend.</p>
