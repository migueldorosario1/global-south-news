<p>The United States and Iran on Wednesday released the full text of a memorandum of understanding that mandates an immediate and permanent ceasefire on all fronts, including Lebanon, and outlines a sweeping framework for normalising relations and economic reconstruction. The agreement, published by The Hindu, details a 60-day window to finalise a binding accord, while providing immediate sanctions relief and unblocking Iranian oil exports.</p>
<p>Under the terms, Washington will begin removing its naval blockade on Iran within 30 days, and Tehran commits to facilitating safe, toll-free passage for commercial vessels through the Strait of Hormuz for 60 days. Iran will also engage Oman and other Gulf states to discuss future administration of the strategic waterway. The United States pledges to release frozen Iranian assets and issue Treasury Department waivers for crude oil and petroleum product exports immediately upon signing.</p>
<p>The deal envisages a $300 billion reconstruction and economic development plan for Iran, with implementation mechanisms to be negotiated as part of the final agreement. All required financial transaction licenses will be granted by Washington. The accord also sets a schedule for terminating all U.S. and United Nations sanctions, including Security Council resolutions, while Iran reaffirms it will not acquire nuclear weapons and will down-blend existing enriched material under IAEA supervision. Both sides agree to maintain the nuclear status quo and refrain from new sanctions or military deployments pending a final deal.</p>
<p>For Global South nations, the agreement could steady volatile energy markets and secure the Strait of Hormuz, through which a third of the world’s seaborne oil transits. The removal of sanctions on Iranian crude—long a lifeline for many developing economies—promises to ease price pressures and diversify supply. The truce in Lebanon and across the region also reduces the risk of broader conflict that has threatened maritime chokepoints critical to Asia and Africa. A final deal, endorsed by a binding UN Security Council resolution, would lock in the cessation of hostilities and provide a durable diplomatic framework, potentially reshaping Middle Eastern geopolitics for the majority world.</p>
