<p>Iranian cruise missiles struck two UAE-flagged oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, killing an Indian crew member and wounding eight others, the United Arab Emirates Ministry of Defence said. The vessels, Mombasa and Al Bahiyah, were hit while transiting Omani territorial waters in the southern lane of the strategic chokepoint.</p>

<p>The ministry confirmed that the fatality occurred aboard the Mombasa, while four of the wounded were in serious condition. Six of the injured were Indian nationals and two were Ukrainian, highlighting the heavy reliance on migrant labour from the Global South in the Gulf’s energy shipping. Fires broke out on both tankers but were brought under control, the statement said.</p>

<p>The UAE condemned the strike as a “blatant attack” and asserted “its full right to respond to this escalation,” adding that it was taking all necessary measures to defend its security. Iran offered no immediate comment on the incident. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations separately reported that a tanker was hit by a projectile 40 nautical miles northeast of Oman’s Qalhat, though it was unclear if this referred to the same event.</p>

<p>Tuesday’s attack marks a sharp escalation in the waterway, which has been a flashpoint since the US and Israel launched military strikes on Iran on February 28. President Donald Trump said on Monday that Washington would reinstate a blockade on Iranian shipping and ensure the strait remains open “for a fee,” while warning of further heavy strikes. Iran’s joint military command responded that the US had no role in determining the waterway’s future.</p>

<p>The Strait of Hormuz is a vital artery through which roughly a fifth of global oil and gas supplies pass daily, directly linking energy security in Asia, Africa, and Latin America to the stability of the Gulf. The strike not only endangers that flow but exacts a human cost borne disproportionately by seafarers from developing nations. Before the conflict erupted, more than 15 million barrels of fuel valued at over $1.2 billion transited the strait each day.</p>
