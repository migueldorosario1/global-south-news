<p>Iranian cruise missiles struck two UAE-flagged oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, killing an Indian crew member and wounding eight others, the United Arab Emirates Ministry of Defence said. The attack on the Mombasa and Al Bahiyah occurred in Omani territorial waters along the southern lane of the strategic chokepoint.</p>

<p>The dead sailor was aboard the Mombasa, the ministry said. Six of the injured were Indian nationals and two were Ukrainian; four sustained serious wounds. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency separately reported a tanker hit by an unknown projectile 40 nautical miles northeast of Oman’s Qalhat, though it was not immediately clear if this referred to the same incident.</p>

<p>The UAE condemned what it called a “blatant attack” and asserted its “full right to respond to this escalation.” It added that the country remained prepared to deal with any threats and would take necessary measures to protect its security and stability.</p>

<p>The incident escalates the U.S.-Iran conflict that began on February 28 with American and Israeli strikes. On Monday, President Donald Trump reinstated a naval blockade on Iranian shipping and threatened further strikes, while Iran’s military command rejected any U.S. role in the waterway. A tentative agreement last month to reopen the strait and pause hostilities now appears in jeopardy.</p>

<p>For the Global South, the disruption of Hormuz—through which a fifth of the world’s oil and gas flows daily—threatens energy security and exposes the vulnerability of migrant labour from developing nations. The attack underscores the human cost for Indian, Ukrainian and other seafarers who keep vital trade routes operating amid deepening geopolitical turmoil.</p>
