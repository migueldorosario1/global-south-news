<p>Pakistan on Thursday accused India of weaponising water resources after New Delhi pushed forward with two river projects on the Chenab River, further straining a critical bilateral treaty already suspended by India.</p>
<p>Foreign ministry spokesperson Tahir Andrabi told reporters the projects — which India had not consulted Islamabad on — would “undermine” the Indus Waters Treaty, the 1960 pact that governs the use of cross-border waterways. “These projects confirm that India seems to weaponise water,” Andrabi said. “This carries dangerous implications not only for Pakistan’s economy but also for regional stability and international peace and security.”</p>
<p>India’s state-owned National Hydroelectric Power Corporation last month issued a tender for a tunnel to divert Chenab water into the Beas basin, while the power ministry in January said it was conducting “sediment removal” at the Salal Power Station on the same river, citing the termination of the treaty. New Delhi insists it is acting within its rights, though it suspended the pact in 2025 after a deadly attack on tourists in Indian-administered Kashmir that it blamed on Pakistan-backed militants — a charge Islamabad denies.</p>
<p>The two nuclear-armed neighbours then exchanged drone, missile and artillery fire that killed nearly 70 people on both sides, marking their worst military escalation in years. Andrabi said the treaty still binds both governments and warned that “any illegal measure to endanger Pakistan’s water, food and economic security as well as the survival and wellbeing of its 250 million people is unacceptable.” Pakistan, he added, “will retain all options necessary” to safeguard its rights, without specifying a response.</p>
<p>The dispute highlights water’s growing role as a flashpoint in a region grappling with climate change and rising populations that strain the agricultural sectors underpinning both economies. India’s foreign ministry last month rejected a ruling by a Hague-based arbitration court that Islamabad said confirmed the treaty remained in effect, with New Delhi insisting its suspension of the pact “remains in force.”</p>
