<p>ST PETERSBURG, June 4 – Laureano Ortega, presidential adviser and special representative of Nicaragua’s co-presidents for relations with Russia, chaired a meeting of the bilateral joint commission here to review progress on health, communications, culture, the economy, sports and security, Nicaraguan officials said.</p>
<p>The talks placed heavy emphasis on the Latin American Institute of Biotechnology Mechnikov, which has operated in the country for more than a decade. Ortega confirmed the institute is producing a seasonal influenza vaccine distributed free of charge to the Nicaraguan population and is working on supplying a vaccine against human papillomavirus. He added that Nicaragua will join Russian-led clinical trials for vaccines against dengue and cancer, deepening a health partnership that already spans years.</p>
<p>“We are developing, manufacturing and producing the flu vaccine that is given free to the Nicaraguan people,” Ortega told Russia’s RT network, as cited by the government-linked website El 19.</p>
<p>The commission also examined the design and financing of a Nuclear Medicine Centre in Nicaragua, backed by Russian state corporation Rosatom, with construction planned for the end of this year. Ortega said the facility would serve not only Nicaraguans but patients from across Central America, touting “enormous potential to guarantee the health of our people and extend these technologies to the entire Central American, Caribbean and Latin American region.”</p>
<p>On trade, Nicaragua signed a comprehensive joint action plan with the Eurasian Economic Union for the period 2026-2029, a step Ortega said would strengthen relations with the bloc’s members. He highlighted existing cooperation with Belarus and expressed Managua’s interest in expanding ties with other union states as it consolidates its observer status. The Nicaraguan delegation also held a separate meeting with Abkhazia’s President Badra Gunba.</p>
