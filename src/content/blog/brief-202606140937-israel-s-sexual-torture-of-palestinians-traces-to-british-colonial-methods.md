<p>An Al Jazeera documentary and accompanying essay have documented that Israel’s systematic use of sexual torture against Palestinian detainees is a direct inheritance of British colonial methods, a practice institutionalised from the state’s founding and maintained as a tool of control.</p>
<p>The findings, detailed in <em>Bodies of Evidence: Israel's Darkest Weapon</em>, draw on survivor testimony and historical research to link contemporary abuse in Israeli detention to counterinsurgency tactics developed by European empires. Survivor Rasmea Odeh testified before a UN committee in 1979 that she was raped with a stick and subjected to electric shocks to her mouth and genitals during a 1969 interrogation in Jerusalem. Former Addameer director Abdel Latif Ghaith, held in an adjacent cell, corroborated witnessing her naked and severely tortured.</p>
<p>“Torture is not new to the Palestinians. Israel has practised torture against the Palestinians since the very beginning of its existence,” said Francesca Albanese, the UN special rapporteur on the occupied Palestinian territories. Albanese identified a direct lineage: Britain exported the emergency regulations and personnel it had used to suppress the Irish insurgency to Mandate Palestine, where those laws were “immediately received and ingrained in the Israeli system.”</p>
<p>The British colonial apparatus that deployed the Black and Tans paramilitary force in Ireland sent approximately 650 of those same men to form the Palestine Gendarmerie in 1922. They brought with them interrogation methods that would outlast the Mandate, including the architecture of administrative detention. During the 1936-1939 Arab Revolt, Britain’s police manuals authorised arrest without warrant, forced entry, and blanket legal indemnity for officers, creating the institutional framework for deniable abuse.</p>
<p>Sexual violence operates not as an aberration but as a calculated mechanism of control, exploiting social stigma to break political will. Kifaya Khraim, international advocacy coordinator at the Women’s Centre for Legal Aid and Counselling, noted that Israeli forces “know of the social stigma, and they use it and they exploit it,” describing accounts of detainees subjected to internal abuse they could not publicly name.</p>
<p>International law classifies such acts as torture prosecutable as war crimes. The historical record confirms a pattern extending from 1948 to recent cases: forced nudity, binding, the presence of relatives, and the deliberate infliction of sexual humiliation remain recurrent methods.</p>
