<p>Iranian cruise missiles struck two Emirati oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, July 14, killing one Indian crew member and wounding eight others, the United Arab Emirates Ministry of Defence said, sharply escalating tensions in the strategic waterway.</p>

<p>The ministry identified the vessels as the Mombasa and Al Bahiyah, which were hit in the southern lane of the strait while transiting Omani territorial waters. One Indian national aboard the Mombasa died, while six Indian and two Ukrainian sailors were injured, four of them seriously. Fires broke out on both tankers but were later brought under control.</p>

<p>The UAE condemned the “blatant attack” and said it retained “its full right to respond”, warning it would take all necessary measures to safeguard its security. The strike came hours after US President Donald Trump announced a reinstated American naval blockade on Iranian shipping and threatened to hit Iran “very hard” following tit-for-tat missile and drone exchanges.</p>

<p>The latest violence marks a dangerous escalation in a conflict that erupted on 28 February when the US and Israel launched attacks on Iran. Iran’s military leadership has rejected any US role in the Gulf, vowing to resist intervention. The stand-off has thrown an interim US-Iranian agreement to reopen the strait and halt hostilities into doubt.</p>

<p>The human cost borne by Indian nationals underscores the vulnerability of Global South workers, who form the backbone of the maritime industry. The Strait of Hormuz, through which about a fifth of global oil trade passes daily, remains a choke point. Prolonged disruption threatens energy prices and economic stability across developing economies heavily reliant on imported fuel.</p>
