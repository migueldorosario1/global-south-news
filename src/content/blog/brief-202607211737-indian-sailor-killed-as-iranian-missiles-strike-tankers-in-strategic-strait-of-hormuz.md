<p><strong>Iranian missiles strike UAE oil tankers, killing Indian sailor</strong></p>
<p>Iranian cruise missiles struck two oil tankers flagged to the United Arab Emirates in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, killing one Indian crew member and wounding eight others. The UAE’s Ministry of Defence said the attack marked the latest escalation in a conflict that has shaken global energy markets.</p>
<p>The tankers, Mombasa and Al Bahiyah, were hit while transiting the southern lane of the strategic waterway in Omani territorial waters. The Indian national who died was aboard the Mombasa. Six Indians and two Ukrainians were wounded, four of them seriously, and fires that broke out on both vessels were brought under control. The ministry did not detail the extent of material damage.</p>
<p>The UAE condemned what it called a “blatant attack” and said it retained “its full right to respond to this escalation”. It added that its forces remained prepared to deal with any threats to the country’s security and stability. There was no immediate comment from Iran.</p>
<p>The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency separately reported that a tanker had been hit by an unknown projectile about 40 nautical miles northeast of Oman’s Qalhat, with a strike to the starboard-side engine room. All crew were safe. It was not immediately clear whether that incident was linked to the UAE’s announcement.</p>
<p>The violence is the latest since the US and Israel launched large-scale military operations against Iran on February 28, plunging the Gulf into open conflict. Before the war, roughly one-fifth of global oil and gas flows — more than 15 million barrels a day worth at least $1.2bn — passed through the Strait of Hormuz.</p>
<p>On Monday, President Donald Trump said the US was reinstating a naval blockade on Iranian shipping and would keep the strait open “for a fee”, warning that American forces would strike Iran again “very hard” in the coming hours. Iran’s top military command rejected any US role in the waterway, insisting that Washington would not be allowed to intervene. The deteriorating security situation has called into question an interim agreement signed last month between Washington and Tehran to reopen the strait and halt hostilities.</p>
