<p>BEIJING — China and Pakistan unveiled a sweeping new consensus to deepen strategic cooperation on Tuesday, following meetings in Beijing between Pakistan’s Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and Chinese President Xi Jinping and Premier Li Qiang. The joint statement outlined plans to accelerate high-quality development of the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor and transform the southwestern port of Gwadar into a regional connectivity hub.</p>
<p>The two sides committed to upgrading the Karakoram Highway and the Khunjerab Pass, the primary overland artery linking the neighbours. In a notable shift, both governments welcomed third-party participation in CPEC projects under an agreed model, signalling an openness to broaden investment in the Belt and Road Initiative’s flagship corridor.</p>
<p>Pakistan pledged targeted security measures to protect Chinese workers and investments, a persistent concern for Beijing following repeated militant attacks on its nationals and projects. The commitment forms part of a broader effort to restore investor confidence and sustain momentum behind the infrastructure partnership.</p>
<p>On regional diplomacy, China expressed appreciation for Pakistan’s role in mediating the temporary ceasefire between the United States and Iran and for hosting talks in Islamabad. Both nations reiterated support for the early adoption of a five-point initiative aimed at restoring peace in West Asia, offering to contribute constructively to the process.</p>
<p>Pakistan reaffirmed its commitment to the one-China principle, stating that Taiwan is an “inalienable” part of China and opposing any form of independence for the island. Taiwan’s government rejects Beijing’s sovereignty claims, insisting its future is a matter for its people.</p>
<p>The statement also addressed Afghanistan, with Pakistan welcoming Chinese efforts to advance dialogue. Both sides opposed the use of any territory by groups such as the Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan and the Eastern Turkistan Islamic Movement to threaten regional security or launch attacks, underscoring shared counterterrorism priorities.</p>
