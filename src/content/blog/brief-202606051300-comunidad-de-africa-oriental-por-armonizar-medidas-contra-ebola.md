<p>The East African Community (EAC) has set up a regional technical working group to co-ordinate the response to the Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak, as the Pandemic Fund’s executive board approved $220.6m in emergency financing for affected and at-risk nations in East and Central Africa.</p>
<p>The working group, composed of experts from EAC member states including the Democratic Republic of Congo, will monitor the outbreak, co-ordinate technical interventions, review epidemiological trends and report periodically to ministers and other bloc decision-making bodies. The decision follows a virtual ministerial meeting held on June 1-2, the EAC said on Thursday.</p>
<p>Ministers urged collective action to prevent cross-border transmission and called on member countries to implement surveillance and control measures at border points, entry sites and porous frontiers in line with World Health Organization recommendations. They also requested a meeting with civil aviation safety and other agencies to harmonise screening protocols, health declaration requirements for travellers and other public health steps.</p>
<p>The EAC stressed the need for real-time sharing of epidemiological data to enable early case detection and support a co-ordinated regional response. Member states were mandated to facilitate the deployment of mobile laboratories and technical experts at strategic locations, including border entry points and identified high-risk zones, and to mobilise additional financial and technical resources.</p>
<p>Ten mobile laboratories are already operational in the region — in DRC, Uganda, Kenya, Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan and Tanzania — to speed up testing of suspected Ebola cases.</p>
<p>In a parallel move, the Pandemic Fund’s executive board approved on Thursday the release of $220.6m to tackle the Bundibugyo strain outbreak. Up to $175.7m will be redirected from existing Fund projects to support responses in nine countries: DRC, South Sudan, Rwanda, Burundi, Tanzania, Zambia, Angola, Kenya and Ethiopia. An additional $44.9m will be made available through accelerated procedures for Uganda, the Central African Republic and the Republic of Congo.</p>
