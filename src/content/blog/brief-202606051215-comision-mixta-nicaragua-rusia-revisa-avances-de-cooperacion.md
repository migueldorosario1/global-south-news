<p>Nicaragua’s presidential adviser Laureano Ortega chaired a joint commission with Russia in St. Petersburg on Wednesday, reviewing cooperation in health, communications, culture, economy, sport and security and highlighting progress in vaccine production and plans for a nuclear medicine centre.</p>
<p>Ortega, who is the special representative for the Nicaraguan co-presidents on Russian affairs, said the Latin American Mechnikov Institute of Biotechnology, operating in Nicaragua for more than a decade, is now manufacturing influenza vaccines distributed free to the population. Both sides are also working on supply of a human papillomavirus vaccine, and Nicaragua is joining clinical trials of Russian-developed vaccines against dengue and cancer, he told Russia’s RT network.</p>
<p>The commission examined design and financing for a Nuclear Medicine Centre in Nicaragua, backed by state corporation Rosatom, with construction expected to begin by the end of this year. Ortega said the facility would serve patients from across Central America and could later extend such technologies to the Caribbean and Latin America.</p>
<p>On the economic front, Nicaragua signed a comprehensive joint action plan with the Eurasian Economic Union for the period 2026-2029, aiming to deepen trade and investment ties with the five-member bloc. Ortega noted existing cooperation with Belarus and expressed Managua’s interest in broadening relations with other EAEU members to strengthen its observer status.</p>
<p>During the visit, the Nicaraguan delegation also met with Badra Gunba, the president of Abkhazia, local media reported.</p>
