<p>The United Arab Emirates Ministry of Defence condemned a "blatant attack" after Iranian cruise missiles struck two Emirati oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz on July 14, 2026, killing one Indian crew member and injuring eight others. The ministry said the tankers, Mombasa and Al Bahiyah, were targeted while in Omani territorial waters, with the dead crew member aboard the Mombasa and six Indians and two Ukrainians among the wounded.</p>

<p>The attack caused material damage to both vessels after fires broke out, though the ministry noted the fires were brought under control. UAE officials emphasized the nation "retained its full right to respond to this escalation" as it remained prepared to deal with any threats to its security. The incident follows heightened tensions since February when the United States and Israel launched attacks on Iran, with President Donald Trump warning U.S. forces would hit Iran again "very hard" after recent exchanges of missile and drone attacks.</p>

<p>The Strait of Hormuz, through which 20% of the world's oil and gas traffic passes, has become increasingly volatile since the conflict began, destabilizing the Gulf region and throwing into question an interim U.S.-Iranian agreement to reopen the waterway and halt hostilities. Iran has not commented on the latest attacks, though its top military command previously stated the U.S. had no role in determining the strait's future.</p>
